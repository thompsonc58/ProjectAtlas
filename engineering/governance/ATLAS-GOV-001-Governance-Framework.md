# Executive Summary

The **Atlas Governance Framework** establishes the principles, organizational structure, decision authority, and operational processes that govern the engineering, maintenance, and continuous improvement of collegiate curriculum within the **Atlas Curriculum Engineering System (CES)**. It serves as the constitutional document for Atlas governance and provides the authoritative foundation upon which all governance standards, engineering processes, templates, policies, and reference implementations are built.

Atlas recognizes curriculum as a complex, interconnected system that benefits from the rigor of engineering principles while remaining firmly grounded in established educational theory and the professional judgment of educators. Rather than replacing existing educational practices, Atlas modernizes curriculum development by integrating proven pedagogical frameworks with systems engineering methodologies, evidence-based decision making, and the responsible use of emerging technologies.

Central to the Atlas philosophy is a **shared governance model** that balances academic authority with collaborative decision making. Faculty serve as the primary stewards of curriculum quality and retain ownership of academic decisions relating to learning outcomes, instructional design, assessment strategies, and curriculum integrity. Curriculum committees, academic leadership, subject matter experts, industry partners, assessment specialists, and institutional stakeholders each contribute clearly defined responsibilities throughout the curriculum engineering lifecycle.

Artificial Intelligence is recognized within Atlas as an **engineering assistant**, not an academic authority. AI may assist faculty by generating draft artifacts, reviewing curriculum for consistency, identifying gaps, analyzing assessment data, and recommending evidence-based improvements. However, all academic decisions, approvals, and governance responsibilities remain the responsibility of qualified human participants operating within the institution's governance structure.

Atlas is intentionally **pedagogy before technology**. Educational objectives, student learning outcomes, and sound instructional practice determine the selection of assessment methods, instructional strategies, and supporting technologies—not the reverse. Atlas embraces established educational theories including, but not limited to:

- Bloom's Taxonomy
- Constructive Alignment
- Backward Design
- Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
- Competency-Based Education
- Authentic Assessment
- Accreditation Standards
- Evidence-Based Teaching Practices

These educational foundations remain central to Atlas and are enhanced through engineering discipline rather than replaced by technology.

The Governance Framework further establishes principles for transparency, accountability, traceability, and continuous improvement. Significant curriculum revisions are expected to be supported through documented evidence and validated through consultation with either an Industry Advisory Board or a qualified Subject Matter Expert (SME) review panel. Every major curriculum decision should be traceable from identified educational or industry need through implementation, assessment, evaluation, and future revision.

As the constitutional governance standard for Atlas, this document establishes the governance relationships among Atlas artifacts, defines institutional conformance expectations, and provides the foundation for curriculum approval workflows, configuration management, document control, release governance, and continuous improvement. Subsequent governance standards derive their authority from the principles and governance model established herein.

Atlas is designed to support institutions of varying sizes, missions, and governance structures. While institutional implementation may differ, all conforming implementations should preserve the core principles of faculty ownership, shared governance, evidence-based decision making, responsible AI assistance, continuous improvement, and academic excellence.

Ultimately, Atlas transforms curriculum from a collection of independent documents into an integrated, living system engineered for quality, adaptability, and long-term sustainability. By combining faculty expertise, proven educational practice, industry relevance, structured engineering methodologies, continuous improvement, and responsible AI assistance, Atlas provides higher education with a modern framework for engineering curriculum while preserving the academic values that define collegiate education.

The result is a governance framework that is transparent, scalable, adaptable, and sustainable—positioning institutions to respond effectively to evolving educational, technological, and workforce demands while maintaining the highest standards of academic quality.

## Atlas Mission

> **Atlas exists to modernize curriculum engineering by integrating proven educational theory, faculty expertise, industry relevance, structured engineering practices, evidence-based continuous improvement, and responsible AI assistance to create adaptable, high-quality collegiate learning experiences.**

## Guiding Principle

> **Pedagogy Before Technology**

Educational goals shall drive curriculum design. Technology—including artificial intelligence—shall be selected only when it meaningfully supports teaching, learning, assessment, accessibility, and student success.
